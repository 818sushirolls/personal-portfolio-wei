## What it is

A classifier trained on the UCI Wine Quality dataset (6,500+ samples across red and
white) that predicts a quality score from physicochemical features like residual
sugar, alcohol, and pH.

The point wasn't to win on accuracy. It was to build a small, honest end-to-end
pipeline I could trust: load, clean, split, fit, evaluate, and compare model
configurations reproducibly.

## How it works

The pipeline is built as reusable data-processing and model-training modules
wrapped around `scikit-learn`. The key pieces are:

1. **Stratified train / validation / test splitting** so the class distribution is
   preserved across folds.
2. **Train-only feature fitting.** Every scaler, PCA decomposition, and polynomial
   expansion is fit on the train fold and only applied to validation and test. No
   information leaks across folds, even by accident.
3. **Model sweep** comparing logistic regression, support vector machines, and
   k-nearest neighbors.
4. **Feature variants** including standardized raw features, PCA-reduced features,
   and polynomial expansions up to degree two.

The combination of model x scaler x feature variant produces 72 configurations,
each trained and evaluated under the same protocol so the comparison is apples to
apples.

## What I learned

The big lesson was about **what the evaluation metric is actually telling you.**
Best test F1 landed at 0.49. Not glamorous, but the dataset is sharply imbalanced,
the labels are noisy and ordinal, and accuracy would have been misleading.
Macro-F1 surfaced the real failure mode: every model could separate "medium" from
the extremes, but struggled at the boundary between adjacent classes.

It was also the first time I treated train / eval discipline as **code** rather
than as intent. The pipeline can't cheat, because the scaler literally doesn't
exist until training. Nothing in the validation step can see test data even by
accident.

## What I'd do next

Treat quality as **ordinal** rather than categorical, which is what it actually
is. There's a whole family of ordinal regression and ordered-classification
techniques I'd want to compare against the multiclass baseline. I'd also
calibrate the predicted probabilities, because right now the models are sharply
overconfident on the medium class.
